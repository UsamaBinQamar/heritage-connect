# Heritage Connect

You are building MotherlandMatch.

This prompt is the Product Constitution. It is binding on every later prompt.

Do not design screens, do not invent pages, do not create database tables, do not generate components, and do not write marketing copy in this step.

Acknowledge the constitution, restate the laws in your own compact checklist, and wait.

WHAT MOTHERLANDMATCH IS

MotherlandMatch is a private, human-led matchmaking operating system.

It primarily serves Africans, people of African descent, and the global African diaspora who are seeking intentional, marriage-minded partnership.

That sentence is positioning, not a database eligibility rule. Do not encode race, nationality, or heritage as a hard signup block unless a later prompt explicitly requires it.

Technology organizes the work. Humans make the introductions.

It is a company system with four core user roles plus visitors, not a dating app with a pretty homepage.

WHAT MOTHERLANDMATCH IS NOT

Not Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, or any swipe product.

Not a public member directory.

Not an algorithmic “soulmate score” product.

Not a member-to-member messenger.

Not a generic luxury template.

Not a form dumped into a cream theme.

Never add:

swiping

likes

feeds

public browse

infinite cards

member-to-member chat

presence dots

“people near you”

ROLES

Visitor: marketing pages only.

Applicant: person filling the private application. Data remains a draft until submitted.

Member: accepted client. Sees only their own profile, application status, introductions shared with them, date feedback, membership, and privacy controls.

Matchmaker: internal operator. Sees assigned clients, searchable member facts and preferences, shortlists, proposals, workflow, and private notes.

Admin: manages operators, verification, payments, deletion requests, analytics, audit, and system-level operations.

THREE ENVIRONMENTS, ONE BRAND

Marketing site — cinematic, editorial, romantic.

Application — private, calm, reassuring, lighter.

Matchmaker OS + Admin — sophisticated and operational. Not romantic decoration.

VOCABULARY

Prefer this vocabulary throughout the product:

application, profile, client, matchmaker, introduction, proposal, candidate, shortlist, membership, verification, preferences, date, feedback, introduction history.

Do not use dating-app vernacular:

swipe, match score, crush, spark, like, discover, explore people, chat now, dating feed.

Other precise operational words are allowed when they are clearer than the list above.

HUMAN MATCHMAKING MODEL

Members do not browse the pool.

A matchmaker identifies candidates, records why, proposes an introduction, collects consent from both sides, and only then reveals what the visibility rules allow.

Interaction after mutual agreement may move off-platform.

Both people return to leave feedback.

The system records outcomes so the next search is better.

Matchmakers work inside MotherlandMatch. They do not claim a profile and disappear into WhatsApp with no record.

DATA LAWS

Separate three layers and never collapse them.

1. Member Facts

What is true about the person.

2. Member Preferences

What they want.

Important preferences support these weights:

must_have

strong_preference

nice_to_have

does_not_matter

3. Matchmaker Intelligence

Private notes, observations, professional judgment, concerns, follow-up information, and search rationale.

Matchmaker intelligence is never visible to applicants or members.

APPLICATION PERSISTENCE LAW

Application persistence is field-level, not page-level.

As soon as email and phone/WhatsApp are valid, create a draft applicant record.

Every field saves.

Going backward is allowed.

Closing the browser must not lose previously saved data.

Progress is measured by completeness across these sections:

Contact

Identity

Life

Relationship

Partner Preferences

Photos

Consent

Do not rely only on “Step 3 of 7.”

Abandoned drafts are recoverable leads where appropriate contact consent exists.

APPLICATION STATES

draft → in_progress → submitted → under_review → accepted | waitlisted | declined | withdrawn

MATCH STATES

Core progression:

proposed → reviewing → mutual_interest → introduced → dated → feedback → closed

Also allow declined or withdrawn at appropriate stages.

Do not assume every proposed introduction reaches mutual interest.

PHOTO LAW

Photos are stored and reviewed.

Visibility is a rule, not a global brand decision.

Supported concepts include:

private_to_matchmakers

reveal_on_proposal

reveal_on_mutual_interest

Blind introduction and photo-first introduction can both exist.

Members must never receive unrestricted access to browse photographs from the member pool.

SEARCH LAW

Matchmaker search supports four concepts:

Include

Require

Exclude

Prefer

Include groups may use OR logic.

Example:

Include: Ghanaian OR Nigerian

Require: wants children, marriage-minded

Exclude: smoker

Prefer: graduate education, lives in North America

Hard requirements affect qualification and warnings. They must not silently erase people from existence.

An authorized matchmaker must be able to inspect an otherwise excluded candidate when professionally appropriate.

The system flags the conflict. It does not pretend the candidate does not exist.

Any override must be:

deliberate

attributable

recorded

never automatic

Search results should eventually explain themselves in human language:

which must-haves are satisfied

which strong preferences align

which conflicts exist

which items require matchmaker review

Never display a fake compatibility percentage.

Human judgment remains final.

PRIVACY LAW

Profiles never appear as a public grid.

Members see only:

their own information

their own account and membership information

introductions deliberately shared with them

their own feedback and relevant introduction history

Matchmaker notes are internal only.

Privacy and deletion requests must be possible later.

Do not design data structures that make legitimate deletion unnecessarily difficult or incomplete.

Do not invent a casual long-lived resume token as the security model.

Use proper authentication and account recovery.

Identity information, photographs, relationship preferences, cultural information, and internal notes may be highly sensitive.

Permissions are part of the architecture, not a later patch.

AUDIT LAW

Important actions must be attributable and reconstructable later.

At minimum, the architecture must eventually support auditing of:

application created

application status changed

matchmaker assigned

candidate shortlisted

introduction proposed

consent recorded

photo visibility changed

override of a hard-requirement conflict

membership changed

payment status changed

deletion request created

deletion request completed

A later prompt will specify the audit architecture.

Do not invent a noisy log of every keystroke now.

Do not skip this requirement.

COMMERCIAL LAW

Do not put payment on the first application screen.

Allow the applicant to meaningfully engage with the application before payment becomes relevant.

Pricing is a business-rule system separate from signup.

Do not assume every applicant pays the same amount.

Women-may-be-free / men-pay is a possible business rule, not a hardcoded assumption in the initial application UI.

Membership tiers that currently exist in product language are:

Curated

Signature

Legacy

Do not invent prices.

Do not invent benefits for these tiers until a later prompt defines them.

BUILD LAW FOR ALL FUTURE PROMPTS

Each later prompt owns one system or one experience.

A later prompt may not:

restyle the whole product

rewrite global navigation

alter unrelated database structures

rename established product concepts

modify completed pages

“improve” unrelated areas

introduce new business rules

add dating-app mechanics

replace working functionality

unless explicitly instructed by that prompt.

If a prompt does not mention a page, system, component, workflow, or data structure, leave it alone.

When implementing a future prompt:

State briefly what will be built.

State what existing areas will not be touched.

Implement only the requested scope.

Preserve working functionality outside that scope.

Do not silently make product-policy decisions.

If a material ambiguity could affect security, privacy, permissions, payments, matchmaking logic, or data architecture, identify it rather than inventing a permanent rule.

Reuse established components and patterns where appropriate.

Keep desktop and mobile behavior intentional.

Preserve this Product Constitution.
